MSARM

Markov-switching autoregressions in R

When the level, volatility or dynamics of a time series change, the regime driving that change may be hidden. MSARM estimates the probability of each regime from the observed series and carries that uncertainty into forecasts.

Supervised by Dr. Ingo Steinke, University of Mannheim.

From series to forecast

Model specification

yt = cst + ∑ φk,st yt−k + σst εt

A hidden Markov chain determines the active regime. The intercept c, each lag coefficient φ and the innovation variance σ² can vary independently.

Estimate with EM

Specify the AR order, regime count and switching mask. EM alternates between estimating latent regime probabilities and updating the model parameters. Multiple random starts and a variance floor help with sensitivity to initialisation and near-degenerate variances.

Infer states

Filtering conditions on observations through the current time. Smoothing uses the entire sample. The fitted object returns smoothed probabilities and fitted values for the most probable smoothed regime.

Forecast uncertainty

Propagate regime probabilities and lag moments for point forecasts. The residual bootstrap simulates future regime transitions and resamples centred, standardised residuals along each path. Quantiles give pointwise prediction intervals.

Regime recovery compared with MSwM

The BSc thesis derives the estimator and compares the packages across 288 simulated processes. The follow-up benchmark uses MSARM 1.0.1.

MSARM 1.0.1 and MSwM 1.5 were fitted to the same 300 simulated series across 25 designs. Both received the generating AR order, number of regimes and switching mask, with five starts and a budget of 250 EM iterations per start. Each package retained its own initialization and convergence rules. A shared conditional Gaussian likelihood and smoother were used to evaluate the fits.

A recovery failure is defined as no finite fit, more than 20% of time points misclassified, or zero recall for an observed regime. Classification error is measured after aligning fitted labels to the true labels. Single-regime collapse assigns every time point to the same regime on a series with multiple observed regimes.

A finite fit that misses a regime

In the selected intercept-switching series clear/1, the true intercepts are −3 and +3. MSARM estimates −2.94 and +2.81 and recovers the regime path at all 159 scored observations. The best MSwM fit estimates two similar intercepts (−0.35 and −0.45), assigns all 159 observations to one regime, and misses 32 observations (20.1%). Under the shared scoring rule, the log-likelihoods are −196.11 and −293.75, respectively. All five MSwM fits assigned every observation to a single regime in this series.

The difference is concentrated in particular designs. Both estimators passed all 16 original full-switching cases, and both struggled when the regimes strongly overlapped. The rates describe the tested designs and fitting budgets. In a supplemental audit, counting variance-floor degeneracy added two MSARM failures, bringing its total to 80/300 (26.7%).

Use MSARM in R

R · Simulate, fit, forecast
library(MSARM)

set.seed(12)
y <- arima.sim(list(ar = 0.5), n = 150)
fit <- MSARM.fit(y, K = 1, N = 2,
                 Switcher = c(TRUE, FALSE, TRUE))
pred <- MSARM.predict(fit, n.ahead = 8,
                      boot = TRUE, L = 1000)
MSARM.plot(pred, conf = TRUE)
MSARM.fit()
Estimate transitions, regime coefficients and smoothed probabilities. Choose a start by log-likelihood, RSS, RCM or normalised entropy.
MSARM.predict()
Return point forecasts and future regime probabilities. Enable boot for the bootstrap mean and prediction quantiles.
MSARM.plot()
Plot the observed series and forecasts. Enable conf to display the bootstrap bands when available.
Lower-level functions

Inference and likelihood. eta_function evaluates conditional densities. zeta_Yt_function calculates filtered and one-step predicted probabilities, zeta_YT_function smooths them, and Log_Likelihood_function evaluates the conditional log-likelihood.

Initialisation and transitions. alpha_0_function, P_0_function and zeta_1_0_function initialise coefficients, transitions and regime weights. P_ij_next_function updates the transition matrix.

Worked EM routines. The three case-specific EM routines expose the estimation steps behind different switching masks. The associated helpers update regression coefficients and, in Example 5, regime variances.
